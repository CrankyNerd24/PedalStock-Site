User Guide

PedalStock keeps track of the parts you have, turns a parts list into a build, and tells you what to order before you start soldering. It's a free web app that runs in any modern browser, on a computer or a phone.

1. Getting started

PedalStock has three main tabs: Inventory for the parts you own, Builds for the pedals you're making, and Low Stock for what's running low or missing.

A good first session:

  1. Add your parts, or import them from a spreadsheet (see importing inventory).
  2. Start a build from a parts list: paste it, import a CSV, scan a schematic, or find one online.
  3. Open Low Stock → Build Shortages to see what you need to order.

Colours: PedalStock comes in PCB Green, with Sky Blue and Classic Orange to choose from. Use Theme at the bottom of any page; the web app and this site share the choice.

Your data stays with you. PedalStock keeps everything in your browser on that computer or phone. There's no account to make. If you want your data in more than one browser, you can sign in (see accounts), or move it with export and import (see backups and moving your data).

2. Inventory

Adding a part

On the Inventory tab, choose Add Part. Pick a category, then fill in what you know:

  • Value, such as 10k, 2N3904, 1N4148 or B100k. For capacitors you enter a capacitance (100nF), a type and an optional voltage rating instead.
  • Name can be left blank: it's made from the value and category, such as "10k Resistor", or just "1/4 Mono Jack" when the value already says what it is.
  • Quantity is how many you have, and low-stock threshold is the count at or below which the part counts as low.
  • Commonly used marks a part you always want in stock. Only commonly used parts appear on the Low Stock list, and they get a star in Inventory.
  • Supplier, price, purchase link and notes are for your own reference.

Values are read the way builders write them, so 4k7, 4.7k and 4.7kΩ are the same resistor, and 1K ohm finds a part saved as 1k.

SMD and through-hole parts

If you keep surface-mount parts as well as through-hole ones, say so in the part's name or notes: a size code like 0805 or 1206, a package like SOT-23 or SOIC-8, or just SMD. Parts only made as SMD (MMBF and MMBT transistors, LL4148, BAT54) are recognized by their number. Everything else counts as through-hole, and so does an SMD part on a through-hole adapter: add adapter or pre-soldered to its name or notes.

An SMD part and a through-hole part with the same value are kept apart: Find Duplicates never pairs them, substitutes never swap one for the other, and the shopping list marks the SMD one, like 10k (0805). A BOM line matches an SMD part only when it says so, like R1 10k 0805. Search smd or through-hole to see one kind.

Finding parts

Inventory is grouped by category. Every section starts collapsed: tap a heading to open it. Searching opens every section so no match is hidden. Search understands values however they're written: 4k7, 4.7k and 4.7k ohm find the same resistor, and 22nF finds a cap saved as 0.022uF. Every word has to match, so 100n film finds only film caps at 100 nF, 25v caps rated 25V, and caps or pots a whole category. Search for a resistor or capacitor value you don't have, like 5k, and the closest values you do have are listed below with how far off they are.

A ▲ next to a part means its quantity is at or below its threshold. Tap a part to see its details, the builds it's used in and, for resistors and capacitors, its substitutes. Long lists have a back-to-top button at the bottom right.

Importing and exporting inventory

On the Inventory tab, use Import CSV… or Export CSV.

Import reads a CSV saved from Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets. Rows that match a part you already have update it, and the rest are added. Parts match by name, or by category and value. Only the columns in your file are changed: leave out price and existing prices stay as they are.

To add a new order on top of what you already have, use an addQuantity column instead of quantity. See file formats for every column, or start from a CSV template.

3. Capacitors

Capacitor rows show the value in its most natural unit as the title, such as "22 nF Ceramic", with the same value in the other units underneath (0.022 µF · 22000 pF). They're sorted smallest first, then by voltage, so your 10µF electrolytics line up 16V, 25V, 50V.

Each capacitor has a type: Film Box, Greenie, Film, Ceramic, Silver Mica, Polarized, Tantalum, Electrolytic or Other. Types matter for substitutes. Film types and ceramics can stand in for each other, and a film cap can take an electrolytic's place, but electrolytic and tantalum caps are never suggested for a film, ceramic or bipolar spot. If a part's type is Other and its name says "box film", "greenie", "MLCC" and so on, PedalStock fills the type in from the name.

The voltage rating is shown in Build Shortages and on build pages, so you can tell a 16V cap from a 25V one at a glance. Parts with the same value but different voltages are treated as different parts.

4. Duplicate parts

Imports and scans can leave you with the same part twice, for example "10k" and "10kΩ Resistor". When PedalStock spots likely duplicates, a "possible duplicates" card appears at the top of Inventory. You can also open the review any time with Find Duplicates… on the Inventory tab.

  • Untick any part in a group that doesn't belong.
  • Tap Merge and choose which part to keep. Quantities are added together, and builds that used the others now use the kept part.
  • Tap Not Duplicates to stop that group appearing again, until another matching part is added. Show Dismissed Groups brings them back.

Capacitors with different voltages or types aren't grouped as duplicates.

5. Starting a build

On the Builds tab, pick how to begin:

OptionUse it for
New BuildAn empty build you'll add parts to by hand.
Find Online…Search the layout and build-doc sites for a pedal, save what you find, and create the build from it. See finding a schematic or BOM online.
Paste BOM…A parts list copied from a forum post, build doc or web page, one part per line: R1 10k, C3 100nF, Q1 2N3904 x2.
Scan Schematic/Layout…A schematic, vero or stripboard layout, or kit build doc as an image, SVG or PDF. See scanning.
Import Builds…A builds file from Export Builds, or a CSV BOM, which becomes a new build named after the file. See the BOM templates.

Every part in the list is matched against your inventory. Parts you don't have are added to inventory with a quantity of 0, so they show up as shortages. Lines for the same part, such as R1 10k and R4 10k, are combined into one row with the total quantity.

Starting a build doesn't take anything out of inventory. Parts are only taken when you tick them as installed, or when you mark the build complete.

6. Scanning schematics and layouts

Scanning reads the printed labels and values on a schematic or layout, such as R1 next to 10k, and pairs each label with the value nearest to it. Build-doc lines that say which value goes where are read along the line: Tone: 25kB, IC2 is CD4046, IC3 & IC4 are TL062, and a range like D1-D4: MA856 covers each part in it. Control names followed by a value, like Rate and Depth 100K Log, give each pot that value, with Log, Lin or Reverse read as the taper. Vero and stripboard layouts that print values on the parts with no labels work too: values printed sideways along the parts are read as well, and each becomes a resistor or capacitor by its unit (10K, 6,8K, 100nF). It works on PNG, JPG, SVG and PDF files. On a phone you can also take a photo of a paper schematic. The file is read in your browser and never uploaded.

PDFs that contain real text, such as kit build docs, are read from that text, which is fast and exact. Scanned PDFs and images go through text recognition, which handles clear, printed labels well; check its work on the review screen.

Finding a schematic or BOM online

Find Online… on the Builds tab, and Find Schematic or BOM Online… on a build, search the sites builders use at once: Tagboard Effects and Effects Layouts for vero and stripboard layouts, PedalPCB, Aion FX, PCBGuitarMania, DIY Fever and General Guitar Gadgets for build docs and BOMs, and Coda Effects for circuit write-ups. On a build, the search starts with the build's name.

Open a result to see the page in a new tab. For a layout image, the quickest way in is to copy it: long-press it on a phone (or right-click it on a computer), choose Copy, come back and tap Paste Image, or press ⌘V or Ctrl+V while the dialog is open. Layout results show thumbnails: tap one to open it full size first. On an iPhone, Safari asks you to confirm the paste the first time.

  • From the Builds tab, the pasted image is scanned into a new build named after your search. Create from a Saved File… does the same with a file you saved, such as a PDF build doc, and Paste Parts List… takes a parts list you copied as text.
  • On a build, the pasted image's parts are added to the build. Scan a Saved File… does the same with a saved file, and Attach a Saved File… keeps one under Schematics & Layouts.

The image itself has to be copied, not its link: these sites don't let other pages download their files, so a link can't be scanned. The scanned image is kept with the build.

For sites that can't be searched directly (madbeanpedals, PedalLayouts, ElectroSmash, GuitarPCB, Fuzz Dog, Love My Switches and the FreeStompBoxes and DIYstompboxes forums) and for the whole web or image search, More places to look opens a web search in a new tab. Only your search words go to these sites; nothing about your inventory does.

Review Scan

Nothing is imported until you've checked it on the Review Scan screen:

  • Recognized: each label with the value it was paired with. Fix any value that was misread. To drop a line, tap ✕.
  • Labels without a value: labels the scan couldn't pair. Type a value to include the part, or leave it blank to skip it.
  • Values without a label: leftover text such as voltages and page numbers, plus real values whose label was missed. Use Use… to give a value to one of those labels, or to add it as a part of a chosen category.

The scanned file is kept with the build, under Schematics & Layouts.

Tips for better scans

  • Use the sharpest version you can find. A PDF from the designer beats a screenshot, and a screenshot beats a photo.
  • Pots labelled by their control name (GAIN, TONE, VOLUME, DEPTH and so on) are recognized when a pot value like B100k is next to them.
  • Values printed with Ω, such as 470Ω, are often read with a stray character. PedalStock fixes the common cases, but check resistor values in the review.
  • A part drawn only as a symbol, with no printed label or value, can't be scanned. Add it from the leftover values or by hand.
  • On layouts, values printed in light text on a dark or grey part, like the white value on an electrolytic cap's grey circle or a trimmer, are often missed. Very small text, like values a few pixels tall on a small screenshot, is missed too: use the largest version of the layout you can find. Count the parts on the board against the review and add any that are missing.

7. Working on a build

Open a build from the Builds tab. At the top you'll find its status, its notes and its schematics and layouts. Tap Edit to rename it or change its circuit type and notes.

Status

A build is Planning, In Progress or Completed. Ticking the first part or stage moves a Planning build to In Progress for you.

Parts

Parts are grouped by category in collapsible sections. A ▲ on a section or row means you don't have enough in stock. On each part row:

  • Tick the circle when you install the part. It comes out of inventory then. Untick it to put it back.
  • − and + change how many the build needs. You can only change the count of a part that isn't ticked, so untick it first if you need to.
  • Tap the part's name (marked ⇄) to use a different part instead, such as a TL062 in place of a TL072. You can pick any part in inventory or create a new one, even if you have none in stock.
  • Remove it from the build with ✕. Anything it took from inventory goes back.

To add parts, use Add Part, Paste More Parts… or Add from Scan…. Parts already in the build have their count raised instead of getting a second row. If a build ends up with the same part on two rows, a Merge Duplicates button appears above the parts list.

Progress

The progress bar counts installed parts and these stages: Parts gathered, Enclosure drilled, Enclosure painted / finished, Board populated, Off-board wiring (pots, jacks, switch, LED, power), Boxed up and Tested. Tick stages as you go.

8. Schematics and layouts

Each build has a Schematics & Layouts section for the files you build from. Scanned files are added here automatically. You can also:

  • Attach a PDF, image or SVG yourself with Attach File…. On a phone you can take a photo instead.
  • Tap a file to open it, or use Download, Rename or ✕.

Deleting a build deletes its files too.

9. Finishing, reopening and deleting

  • Mark Build Complete takes any parts you haven't ticked out of inventory, ticks every stage and locks the build.
  • Reopen Build moves a completed build back to In Progress. You choose whether to return its parts to inventory or keep inventory as it is.
  • Delete Build: if the build has taken parts, you choose whether to return them to inventory or keep them as used.

10. Low Stock and Build Shortages

The Low Stock tab has two lists:

  • Low Stock: commonly used parts at or below their low-stock threshold.
  • Build Shortages: everything your Planning and In Progress builds need more of than you have. Each row shows the part, how many you have and need, how many you're short, and which builds need it.

Shortages are added up across all open builds. If two builds each need five of a part and you have eight, each build looks covered on its own, but you're two short overall. Use the Build menu at the top to see one pedal at a time. The web version remembers your choice.

To order, open Shopping List…. It lists what you're short of grouped by category, smallest value first, with the quantity first on each line. Choose how to round up: Exact, Pack Sizes (resistors, diodes and LEDs in 10s, capacitors and transistors in 5s) or +1 Spare on small parts, and whether to note which builds need each part. Then copy it or share it. It follows the Build menu, so you can make a list for one pedal.

Tap a shortage to use a part you already have instead (see substitutes).

11. Substitutes

When a resistor, capacitor, pot, diode, LED, transistor, op-amp or charge pump is short, PedalStock suggests parts you have that will do the job. For resistors and capacitors, best first:

  1. The same value in another part: a capacitor of an interchangeable type (box film, greenies and other film first for most values, ceramic or silver mica first below 1nF), or the same cap with a higher voltage rating.
  2. A nearby value within tolerance, closest first: a 10.2k for a 10k, a 47 nF for a 50 nF.
  3. Two parts together: resistors in series (4.7k + 5.6k for 10k) or two equal ones in parallel (2 × 20k for 10k); capacitors in parallel (2 × 47 nF for 100 nF) or two equal ones in series.

The tolerance sets how close is close enough, and you choose it under Substitutes on any resistor or capacitor:

  • Tight: resistors within 1%; film, ceramic and mica caps within 5% or ±1 pF; electrolytics within 10%; pots within 10%.
  • Standard (the default): resistors 5%; caps 10% or ±2 pF; electrolytics 20%; pots 20%. About what the parts themselves are rated to.
  • Loose: resistors 10%; caps 20% or ±5 pF; electrolytics 50%; pots 30%, and pots of another taper.

Small caps get whichever is wider, the percentage or the pF, because a percentage of a tiny value is smaller than any real difference: at Standard a 12p can stand in for a 10p, and a 20p for a 22p. Caps that small usually set a treble cutoff or keep a stage stable, and a couple of pF either way won't be heard.

A value a few percent off moves the gain or frequency it sets by about the same few percent, which you'll rarely hear. Stay tight on parts that set a frequency you care about, like tone stacks and LFO timing. Capacitor substitutes always keep the voltage rating at or above the original's, and never put a polarized cap (electrolytic or tantalum) where a non-polarized one goes; a film cap can stand in for an electrolytic, and a bipolar cap for either polarized kind.

A short part on a build shows a one-tap Use … instead suggestion when you have enough of a substitute, and each part page lists what can stand in for it. Two-part substitutes put both parts on the build, so a 10k needed twice becomes four 20k resistors. Two parts in series sit end to end in one part's place (stand them up, or bridge the holes on vero); in parallel they share the same two holes.

Op-amps

Op-amps with the same package and pinout drop into each other's sockets, so PedalStock suggests them too. It knows the common single (TL071, TL081, OPA134, NE5534…), dual (TL072, TL082, 4558, NE5532, OPA2134, LM358…) and quad (TL074, TL084, LM324…) chips under any maker's prefix or package suffix, so JRC4558D, RC4558P and NJM4558 are all the 4558. The same chip under another number comes first, then chips with the same kind of inputs (JFET or bipolar), then the rest, with a note where a swap needs care: the LM358's crossover distortion, the NE5534's compensation pins, the TL06x's low power. Each op-amp sounds a little different, which is why a socket is worth fitting. Search an op-amp, like TL072, to see every chip you have that fits its socket.

Diodes and LEDs

What makes a diode the right one depends on its job in the circuit:

  • Clipping: a pair to ground after a gain stage (hard clipping, as in a Distortion+ or RAT), or in an op-amp's feedback loop (soft clipping, as in a Tube Screamer). The forward voltage sets where it clips: the lower it is, the earlier the signal flattens, so the more it distorts and the quieter the pedal. The knee sets how smoothly it gets there.
  • Power protection: one diode at the power input so a reversed supply can't do damage. It only has to carry the pedal's current, so any 1A diode works. A Schottky (1N5817, 1N5819) drops less voltage than a 1N4001.
  • Charge pumps and rectifiers: voltage doublers, inverters and envelope followers. Current and speed matter more than the exact voltage.

The families PedalStock knows, roughly from earliest to latest clipping:

  • Germanium (1N34A, 1N60, 1N270, OA90, OA91, D9): about 0.3V, with the softest knee and some leakage. Each one varies, and many "1N60" sold today are really Schottky (1N60P).
  • Schottky (BAT41, BAT42/43, BAT46, BAT85, BAT54, 1N5711, and the 1A 1N5817–1N5819): about 0.2–0.4V with a harder knee. The usual stand-in for germanium.
  • Silicon (1N4148, 1N914, 1N4448, which are interchangeable): about 0.6V. The rectifiers (1N4001–1N4007, 1N5408) clip at about the same voltage with a slightly softer knee.
  • Zeners (1N47xx, 1N52xx, BZX55/79): matched by their voltage. A back-to-back pair clips at the zener voltage plus about 0.6V.
  • LEDs: about 1.8V for red, 2.0V for orange and yellow, 2.1V for green, and 3V for blue, white, pure green and pink. That's much later than any diode, so a pedal clipping on LEDs is louder and cleaner.

The tolerance decides how far a swap may go. Tight offers only the same family (germanium for germanium, red LED for red LED). Standard adds another family that clips at about the same voltage (Schottky for germanium, a 1N4001 for a 1N4148), LEDs within 0.3V, and any 1A diode for another. Loose offers any diode or LED, and says whether it will clip earlier or later. A small signal diode offered for a 1A diode is flagged as fine for clipping but not for power protection or a charge pump. As a power indicator, any colour of LED works.

Diode voltages are typical at the small currents in a pedal, not datasheet figures, and two diodes of the same type can differ, germanium most of all. Clipping diodes are worth socketing so you can try them by ear.

Transistors

A transistor swap starts with its type. NPN never stands in for PNP, and a bipolar transistor never for a JFET or MOSFET: the circuit would need rebuilding. Within a type, what matters depends on the transistor's job:

  • Fuzz and boost stages: gain (hFE) shapes how much it distorts and how loud it is, and germanium fuzzes are biased for their transistors.
  • Buffers, switching and LED drivers: almost any transistor of the same type works.
  • JFET boosts and buffers: biased for the JFET's pinch-off voltage. After a swap, set the drain to about half the supply with the trimmer, or change the source or drain resistor.

PedalStock knows the common pedal transistors:

  • Silicon NPN: 2N3904, 2N2222, 2N4401, 2N5088, 2N5089, 2N5210, 2N5133, MPSA18, BC546–BC550, BC337, BC107–BC109, 2SC1815, 2SC828, and the MPSA13/14 Darlingtons.
  • Silicon PNP: 2N3906, 2N2907, 2N4403, 2N5087, BC556–BC560, BC327, 2SA1015, and the MPSA63/64 Darlingtons.
  • Germanium: AC128, NKT275, OC44, OC71, OC81, 2N404, 2N527, 2N1305, AC125, AC188, GT308, MP42, AC127, AC187 and 2N1306.
  • JFETs: J201, J111–J113, 2N5457, 2N5458, MPF102, BF245, 2SK30A and 2N3819.
  • MOSFETs: BS170 and 2N7000.

It reads gain grades from the part number (BC547B is 200–450, 2SC1815GR 200–400). If you measure your transistors, write it in the name or notes, like hFE 85, and it's used instead. Within 15% counts as a match, which helps when picking germanium for a fuzz.

Suggestions say what changes. A different gain means more or less output and distortion in a fuzz or boost. A different pinch-off means re-biasing. A different pinout says how to fit it: "turn it around" when it's the same order reversed (EBC for CBE, or a 2N7000 for a BS170), "cross the legs" otherwise (EBC for ECB). Where a pinout isn't certain, as with metal cans, germanium, the BF245 and the 2SK30A, it says to check it against the layout. The other JFET pinouts and pinch-off ranges are taken from the makers' datasheets: J201, J111–J113, 2N5457, 2N5458 and MPF102 are drain-source-gate, the 2N3819 drain-gate-source.

Tight offers the same type with overlapping gain or pinch-off. Standard offers any transistor of the same type and material. Loose adds germanium for silicon or the reverse, and Darlingtons for ordinary transistors, both flagged as needing different bias. Pinouts vary between makers' versions of the same part, so check the datasheet of the one you have.

Charge pumps

The 7660-style charge pumps that make −9V or 18V in a pedal share one pinout: ICL7660, ICL7660S, TC7660, TC7660S, TC1044S, MAX1044, LTC1044, LTC1144 and MAX660. What differs is the highest supply each takes, so PedalStock offers the highest-rated first and says when one tops out at 9V or 10V and can't run from a 12V supply. The MAX660 is rated to 5.5V and can't run a 9V pedal at all. Ratings vary between makers' versions, so check the datasheet for anything above 9V.

Pots

PedalStock reads a pot's value and taper however it's written (B100k, 100kB, 100k lin, A500k log), and whether it's dual-gang, a trimmer, or a given size like 16mm, from its value, name or notes. It suggests pots of the same taper within the pot tolerance: pots are rated ±20% themselves, so a B470k and a B500k are the same pot in practice. A dual-gang pot can stand in for a single (wire up one gang), never the other way round, and a trimmer only for a trimmer. A different size gets a note to check it fits. Pots of another taper are offered only at Loose, since log, linear and reverse log make the knob sweep differently.

From Build Shortages, or by tapping a part's name on a build, you can pick any part as the replacement. That includes other ICs and transistors, parts you have none of, or a new part. You choose which builds the swap applies to, and a note is added to each build.

12. Backups and moving your data

Your data lives in the browser on one computer or phone, and clearing that browser's site data deletes it, so export now and then, or sign in to keep your inventory and builds in an account.

To move everything to another browser or device:

  1. Export your inventory with Export CSV on the Inventory tab.
  2. Export your builds with Export Builds on the Builds tab. The file includes each build's parts, progress, notes and attached files.
  3. On the other device, import the inventory first, then the builds, so each build's parts match up.

Importing the same builds file twice doesn't create copies: builds that are already there are skipped.

Accounts

Signing in is optional. Choose Sign In at the right end of the Inventory, Builds and Low Stock tabs, then continue with Google or Apple, have a sign-in link emailed to you, or use an email and password. Once you're signed in:

  • Your inventory and builds save to your account as you work, and appear in any browser you sign in on. Changes made in another browser show up straight away.
  • The Account button's dot shows the state: green when everything's saved, blue while saving, orange when you're offline (changes save when you reconnect).
  • Attached files aren't uploaded yet; they stay in the browser you attached them in.

The first time you sign in on a browser that already has data, and your account does too, you choose: Merge Them (parts in both are kept once, with the account's stock counts), Use My Account's Data, or Use This Browser's Data.

Sign Out removes that browser's copy; your data stays in your account. Delete Account deletes your account and everything saved in it. See the privacy policy for what's stored.

13. File and BOM formats

CSV templates

Start from a template: download it, open it in Numbers, Excel or Google Sheets, replace the example rows with your own, and save or export it as CSV.

  • Inventory template Every inventory column, with example parts. Use with Import CSV… on the Inventory tab.
  • Inventory order template Adds a new order on top of what you have, using addQuantity.
  • BOM template, PedalStock columns One row per part with category and quantity. Use with Import Builds… on the Builds tab.
  • BOM template, by part label The usual Designator, Value, Qty layout, as many kits and design tools export it.

A BOM import names the new build after the file, so rename the file to the pedal's name first, for example Fuzz Face.csv.

Inventory CSV

A header row is required. Column names aren't case-sensitive, and spaces and underscores are ignored.

name, categoryRequired. Category is one of Resistor, Capacitor, Potentiometer, IC, Transistor, Diode, LED, Knob, Footswitch, Jack, Enclosure, Wire, PCB, Hardware or Other.
